## First application

Ordinary nonexempt applicants use the online application, meet the statutory age/status/state-connection requirements, pass the office-approved examination and submit the completed signed notarized oath. The current instructions direct preparing the oath before the online form. The examination is open book, directs upload of only the answer sheet and requires retaking when more than six answers are incorrect. The supplemental pack does not introduce a new exam time limit.

## Ordinary renewal and endorsement renewal

Current Rule 9-1(a) and February 2026 instructions specify one approved ordinary CE hour unless exempt. The special endorsement adds one hour related to electronic or remote acts or both. The current examination PDF still contains a multiple-choice ordinary CE question offering two hours rather than the current one-hour answer. This is a real internal official-material inconsistency. This pack uses the current administrative rule and dedicated renewal instructions for learning requirements and does not import the stale exam item into ordinary renewal guidance. The statute permits a rule-defined requirement not exceeding two hours, which accommodates the current ordinary-plus-specialty structure.

## Exemptions and scope

Rules 4-3 and 6-1 identify different treatment for judiciary-related employees, Vermont attorneys in good standing, town clerks/assistants, justices of the peace and law-enforcement-related employees. Ordinary examination, CE and fee exemptions are not interchangeable. The rule expressly preserves commissioning for judiciary employees, and the general ordinary exemptions do not extend to endorsements except for the law-enforcement-related official-duty exception. Outside-scope judiciary work requires full compliance under Rule 4-3(a)(ii).

Section 5305(a)(1)(A) uses broader words exempting qualifying judiciary-related official-duty employees from chapter requirements while retaining specified commission application requirements. Rule 4-3(a) operationally enumerates examination, fee and ordinary CE exemptions. The content explicitly asks what the administrative rule lists and avoids asserting that the statute lacks broader exemption wording. Application of additional official-duty exemption consequences beyond those taught should be checked against both texts. Section 5305(a)(2)(B) now explicitly includes the Department of Corrections even though the May 2026 examination's quoted list omits that department. The current statutory text is used.

## Remote and electronic distinction

Either an electronic record with physical appearance or a remotely located individual triggers the special endorsement, subject to the specified exemption. The notary is physically in Vermont. Electronic execution requires the valid third-party digital certificate and tamper-evident signature association. Remote acts additionally require actual identity route, same-record confirmation, communication, certificate indication and audiovisual recording. The paper-record statutory declaration procedure has its own sending, receipt, certificate and effective-date details.

Rule 8-2(f) sets at least seven years of audiovisual-record retention. Section 5379(k) gives a ten-year default unless a different period is set by rule, so these are reconcilable. The current rule supplies the seven-year learning requirement. KBA numbers in questions and cards concern the signer's remote identity quiz, separately from the commissioning exam. Questions cover five-question/five-choice minimums, eighty percent, two minutes, the single twenty-four-hour retake, forty-percent replacement and post-second-failure restriction.

## Vermont-specific boundaries

The rules authorize the particular tangible-copy-of-electronic-record certification, with tamper-evidence assessment, personal or supervised printing and a prohibition after detected errors. The pack does not import general paper-to-paper authority from other states. Physical official stamps are optional, while the required certificate information remains necessary. Chosen stamps omit the State Seal and satisfy the minimum content and copyability requirements. Physical-stamp loss or theft requires prompt office notice within forty-eight hours, while electronic-credential theft requires immediate notice.
