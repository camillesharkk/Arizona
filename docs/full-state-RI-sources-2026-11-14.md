# Rhode Island official-source and content audit

Checked November 14, 2026. Authored for the current main Notary Prep repository only.

## Commission process verified from primary sources

- An ordinary applicant must be at least 18, be a U.S. citizen or permanent legal resident, read and write English, have Rhode Island residence, employment or practice, be free of applicable disqualification, and demonstrate sufficient knowledge under section 42-30.1-15(b).
- Section 15(c) supplies a professional-credential route for the specified Rhode Island bar members and CPAs regardless of residence. The current form requests profession-specific numbers and unexpired membership evidence. Section 15(d) supplies identified public-office exceptions, including fee and oath provisions. The ordinary commercial applicant workflow should not be generalized to every public office.
- The SOS first and renewal pages recommend reviewing the manual, statute and Standards, attending free training and achieving at least 80% on the Knowledge Assessment. Their wording uses “should.” The statute requires sufficient knowledge. The April 2025 application instructions likewise say applicants should take training and the assessment. The content therefore uses recommendedAssessment and describes the published 80% knowledge target without asserting a mandatory proctored examination.
- The official assessment link resolved to a ClassMarker candidate-information entry. Candidate information was not submitted, and no public question count, time limit or separate credential was verified. The official item-count, minutes and credential fields remain null. The site's comprehensive practice settings must be described as site settings.
- The ordinary first and renewal processes require the original two application pages, original signatures and an oath before a Rhode Island notary. Submission is by mail or in person. Email applications are rejected. The ordinary nonrefundable fee is $80 and the commission term is four years.
- The reviewed ordinary application form, section 15 and SOS workflow specify no bond filing step. The verified oath is part of the pre-submission application. This material does not invent a separate county recording deadline.
- The official form, revised April 2025, accepts mailed check or in-person cash, credit card or check. The web pages mention check or money order. The content states the fee and physical submission without claiming the webpage payment wording is exhaustive.
- The SOS states normal processing of a completed file is 3-5 business days. The form says the certificate arrives in approximately a week. These are estimates rather than appointment guarantees.
- The renewal page says the notary ID is permanent. A completed renewal submitted before expiration preserves the month and day. Section 15(g) and the current renewal page permit the unreappointed notary to officiate for 30 days after expiration. The content expressly limits this holdover. The manual says a renewal more than 30 days late is treated as a new applicant for qualification purposes and includes the knowledge assessment.

## Electronic and remote process verified

- Current RI commission, approved solution provider, electronic signature and stamp samples, information update and Department confirmation precede digital acts. The state update has no filing fee. Vendor services may cost money. Complete provider training.
- In-person electronic notarization and remote online notarization are distinct presence modes. The remote notary must be physically in Rhode Island. Ordinary Zoom, FaceTime and similar meeting applications do not establish the approved-provider procedure.
- Section 12.1 recognizes personal knowledge, a qualifying credible witness or at least two types of identity proofing. It also requires same-record confirmation, an audiovisual recording, the certificate technology indication and default retention for at least ten years. A provider's common ID-plus-KBA process is not described as the statute's sole identity route.
- For a signer outside the United States, section 12.1(b)(4) requires the specified U.S. connection and that the act is not prohibited in the foreign state. The learning content does not invent country-specific eligibility or a remote qualification examination.

## Discrepancies and editorial treatment

1. Section 6 permits the listed ID current or expired no more than three years and uses signature OR photograph for the other-government-ID category. Manual page 19 starts with narrower current/photo-and-signature wording and later states the three-year window. Questions about statutory categories follow the legislature's text and identify the section precisely.
2. Section 20.1 requires fee disclosure before performance and sets a $25 maximum per document or notarization. Manual page 6 uses posting/upon-request language. Fee questions follow the stronger explicit statutory prior-disclosure rule.
3. Section 8 says a physically unable individual may direct another person OTHER THAN the notarial officer to sign, with the required annotation. Standards section 3(h) and manual page 15 describe a different process where the notary signs with witnesses. The bank tests the statute's express directed-other-person route. The guide and card disclose the conflict. There is no synthesized quiz rule merging these inconsistent procedures.
4. Section 12.1 applies its foreign-location safeguards to signers outside the United States. Standards section 8 and the remote guide use outside-Rhode-Island wording. The bank addresses signers outside the United States and avoids the unresolved intermediate-location edge case.
5. The remote guide and Standards contain mailed-paper steps and later certificate completion, while section 12.2 requires contemporaneous certificate execution. The current SOS digital comparison emphasizes digital documents and signatures. No bank question or detailed how-to article claims to resolve remote-paper timing. Seek Department clarification for a real transaction requiring that path.
6. Manual page 19 contains an absolute physical-presence statement. Current sections 5 and 12.1 and the SOS remote pages expressly allow compliant communication technology. The material distinguishes traditional in-person procedures from authorized RON.
7. Traditional journals are recommended under Standards section 3(e), with seven-year end-of-term retention recommended by section 7. Remote recordings are a separate statutory duty with a ten-year default. The material keeps the applicable duties clear.
