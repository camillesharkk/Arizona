# Rhode Island practice update on identity proofing

We updated two Rhode Island practice explanations and the related study materials to show when a notary may decline a request during identity proofing.

Since June 10, 2026, Rhode Island law has expressly allowed a notarial officer to decline if the signer provides too little information to complete identity proofing under the applicable statute or does not agree to pay for it. The separate grounds involving capacity and a knowing, voluntary signature still apply.

Look for RI-162 on documenting an act that was declined, RI-195 on identity proofing for a remote signer, and the access and digital chapters of our [Rhode Island Study Guide](https://arizonanotaryprep.com/rhode-island/study-guide/). The update adds context to the explanations. The answers to both questions are unchanged.

[Rhode Island Public Laws 2026, chapter 54, sections 2–3](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/PublicLaws/law26/law26054.htm)
