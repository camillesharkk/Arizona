## Ordinary first commission

The source-backed route includes eligibility, approved ordinary learning and examination, a prescribed notarized oath, $10,000 assurance, and a nonrefundable $30 application fee. Another current authorized New Mexico officer notarizes the oath. Another current New Mexico notary notarizes the applicant's principal bond signature. The applicant cannot notarize the applicant's own signature.

12.9.3.8(C) contains two conditions for providing the ordinary official-stamp copy. It must be provided within forty-five days of commissioning and before the first act. The outer deadline does not authorize intervening unregistered acts.

12.9.3.10(C) supplies the eighty-percent ordinary passing threshold. The opened official material does not establish a question count or time allowance. Those specifications remain unknown.

## Renewal

12.9.3.8(A)(1) permits the recorded-prior-passage exemption subject to both exceptions. Retraining and examination are required when the commission expired more than one year or SOS determines that substantial statutory or rule changes require them. The handbook's post-January1,2022 learning context is also mentioned in the guide.

The ordinary commission term is four years. The handbook permits renewal submission as soon as one month before expiration. Renewal does not authorize acts during an expired-commission gap.

## Remote and electronic practice

12.9.4.8 requires SOS approval before RON and permits simultaneous ordinary-commission and remote applications. Its $75 fee, listed approved intended systems, authentication instructions, and record-storage explanation are separate from the ordinary application. Ordinary commissioning does not alone authorize RON.

12.9.4.8(C) and .13(A) require the electronic-stamp copy within forty-five days and receipt before the first RON. The officer must be physically in New Mexico. Provider active status is checked for each act. A newly adopted system is reported to SOS.

The RON examination threshold is eighty percent under .9(C). The principal's KBA has different specifications under .11(B), including at least five questions, five choices, eighty-percent correct within two minutes, twenty-four-hour restriction after two failures, forty-percent replacement on retake, and no officer access to the questions or answers. No licensing-exam length is inferred from KBA.

## Journal and custody details

Current 12.9.3 journal provisions are at .15, after the 2023 renumbering. RULONA automatic-officer provisions are at .16. The RON rule's older internal reference to .16 for journals is stale. Content uses the current .15 journal location.

The 2023 statute permits paper for tangible acts and electronic journal for electronic acts. Entries are contemporaneous and carry the required contents. Ten-year journal retention runs after the last recorded act. Authorized transfer and location notice remain relevant after resignation, revocation, or suspension. Employer copies and officer sole control are supported by current 12.9.3.15(B)-(D).

Prompt journal notice is confined to the verified lost-or-stolen scope in 14-14A-18(D) and 12.9.3.15(F). Electronic recoverability after malfunction or crash is a separate requirement under .15(E). This content does not add a legal prompt-notice category for every damaged or temporarily inaccessible device.

RON recordings use protected open format and exclude images of the record being notarized under 12.9.4.14(A). Statutory recording retention is at least ten years after recording, absent another adopted period. Repository termination provisions and death/incapacity options are addressed separately from journal custody.
