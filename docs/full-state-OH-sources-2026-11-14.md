## Ordinary first application

The pack teaches the ordinary nonattorney route with age eighteen, Ohio legal residence unless the actual attorney exception applies, current BCI evidence within six months, authorized three-hour education and testing, electronic filing and the post-approval office oath before duties. Peace officers and Ohio attorneys have the expressly stated application BCI exception. The current Chapter 4796 recognition routes are not analyzed as a replacement for ordinary procedures in this pack.

No uniform statewide commission-exam question count, timer or passing percentage is introduced. Rule 111:6-1-02(F) requires multiple curriculum tests rather than one uniform test circulating statewide. Ordinary retake timing uses at least thirty days after the last exam and the BCI-based six-month outer limit. A second failure triggers the stated restart. Provider education fees and state filing fees are distinguished.

## Attorney and renewal distinctions

The attorney ordinary education/test distinctions depend on first commissioning before or after September 20, 2019. Ohio attorneys have the stated BCI exception and continuing ordinary term conditioned on the Ohio connection, good standing and no revocation. These do not eliminate the RON course/test or Ohio residency and physical-location conditions.

Current nonattorney ordinary renewal requires one authorized hour and no test under the SOS filing table and Rule 03(C). Ordinary renewal opens three months before expiration. Expiration before renewal submission leads to a new application under 147.031(D). Known expiration or resignation does not permit further acts. Section 147.12's validity effect is explained separately from the prohibition and consequences in 147.10-.11.

The SOS page uses broad introductory language saying applicants complete education and testing. Its specific filing-type table correctly identifies ordinary attorney and renewal test exceptions. The pack uses the specific table together with the statute and Rule 03 instead of converting that broad overview into universal testing.

## Current 2025 fee and certificate rules

Section 147.08 currently allows five dollars non-online, thirty dollars online and up to ten dollars total technology charge per online session. Basic fees are not per signature and the ordinary and online basic categories are not stacked. Reasonable travel is agreed before the act. Failed identity can still permit the capped technology fee, without changing the identity result. The pack does not reuse a former twenty-five-dollar online maximum or another state's signature fee units.

Ohio prohibits direct original/true-copy certification under 147.141(A)(5). Section (B) preserves the holder's notarized written statement. The online-journal copy exception and section 147.591 authenticator route are described in their specific scope. The authenticator certificate cannot use electronic signing or notarization, including RON, despite its electronic source record.

Ohio prohibits post-completion certificate amendment or alteration. The pack distinguishes this from 147.542(B)'s duty to provide a correct wrong-act certificate free of charge. It does not import Wyoming-style original-officer pen-edit authorization. Nonattorneys can explain acknowledgment versus jurat procedure and do not advise which best fits a legal situation or decide a power-of-attorney document's validity.

## RON and electronic-record safeguards

First RON authorization requires ordinary authority, Ohio residency, the approved two-hour course/test and written state authorization. In-person electronic notarization under 147.591 is distinct from remote online appearance. The certificate describes the applicable method. Depositions are excluded from the online act authority. An online notary remains physically in Ohio, while foreign principal conditions include the not-known-prohibited test and listed U.S. nexus.

The June 2025 Rule 05 uses an unexpired government credential with photograph and signature for the technological route, credential analysis and identity proofing. It permits personal knowledge and the qualifying single credible witness alternatives. The KBA provisions are principal identity specifications, not commissioning-exam specifications. They include five-question/five-choice minima, eighty percent and two minutes. The rule contains both two retakes within forty-eight hours after the first failure and a no-retry-with-same-notary-or-service restriction within twenty-four hours after the second failure. Content explicitly applies both rather than inferring an immediate third attempt is permitted.

Electronic journals have required fields and cannot contain social security numbers. The statute requires recording, backups, access safeguards and incident notices. Ordinary employer retention differs from a compliant agreed approved-repository arrangement. Expiration transfer and the receiving Secretary/repository ten-year period are identified accurately, and effective renewal begins a new journal. This is not presented as a universal ten-year-from-each-act rule. Attorney journal access and privilege qualifications are retained.
