## First appointment

Ordinary first applicants complete a certified course of at least 180 instructional minutes and the 50-question randomized final with at least 85%. Final bank minimum is 100 questions, with no more than half the administered final true-or-false. Retests use different questions and three failures constitute failure of the course. Application follows passing within two years. No uniform final-examination time limit was verified. The 180-minute instruction period is not labeled an exam clock.

Residents normally apply online with the prescribed current Illinois identification, completion certificate and bond/oath documents. Approved hardship cases may use paper, while qualifying nonresidents use the nonresident paper route. The ordinary bond is $5,000. Approval and acquisition of the required seal precede acting. The content preserves the actual effective commission date as the authority date.

## Renewal and disclosed source discrepancy

Resident terms generally last four years and qualifying nonresident terms one year. The special education/examination exemption in the 2025 amendment is limited to qualifying renewals by specified attorneys, judges and qualifying employees with the required signed statement and current-law reading. It is not presented as a blanket first-application exception.

The online instructions advertise a 90-day renewal opportunity while published Rule 176.350 describes 60 days. The guide, card and scenario explicitly disclose the discrepancy and direct confirmation through the official portal or office. The content does not silently choose one window or use it to extend expired authority. Expiration has no grace period in the official service guidance.

## Journal exception and custody precision

The 2025 text expressly retains §176.900(f). A notary employed by an attorney or law firm need not journal employment notarizations if the attorney or firm maintains copies. Journals solely of that employment work belong to the employing attorney or firm. The privilege protection is preserved. Every general employer-custody scenario is scoped to ordinary employers with no law-firm exception, and the guide distinguishes outside private acts.

The candidate nomination-document exception in §176.900(g)–(h) and Act §3-107(f) is separately explained. It applies to the listed documents and the prescribed candidate relationship. It does not remove identification, appearance or the actual ceremony.

The paper journal seven-year retention period is described with the governmental-employment exception in §176.920(h). Required fields, preprinted numbering, binding, no-charge fees, prohibited ID numbers/biometrics/electronic signatures, privacy redaction and limited requested-line-item disclosure use their actual rule or statute references.

## Remote paper and electronic notarization

Remote paper acts under §6-102.5 are separated from Article VI-A electronic notarization. Paper transmission, 24-hour return and restart requirements are identified by their own provisions. No generic videoconference is described as sufficient by itself.

Electronic notarization requires the electronic commission and approved system. The notary is physically within Illinois and the venue reflects the actual Illinois jurisdiction. The abroad-principal provision is limited to the United States connections stated in §3-105(c). No extra foreign-law condition was invented from another state's RON law.

Electronic documentary identity questions follow §6A-103(b)(2) and Rule176.835. KBA minimum five questions, five choices each, 80%, two minutes and the specified retake limits were read directly in the adopted text. These are kept distinct from the commissioning final. The notary cannot see or record the KBA questions or answers. Electronic recording retention is at least seven years regardless of act completion under §6A-104(d). Seal-compromise notice uses the next-business-day recipients in §3-101(d)(2).

## Source limitations and conservative choices

Current ILGA full-act and direct administrative-code URLs returned server errors during this review. The accessible official SOS handbook reproduces the Act, and the adopted Register PDFs provide the actual rule language. The codification index checked during research still identified the 2023 and 2025 amendments. The materials therefore use the accessible official primary publications and name their dates and section pinpoints.

Ordinary credible-witness questions use the explicit personally-known-witness wording of Act §6-102(d)(2). Remote rules contain wording that differs between provisions, so the new content avoids testing a broader ordinary witness-ID alternative or presenting a disputed electronic credible-witness shortcut. The content avoids unsupported name-change/county-move simplifications and unverified uniform exam time claims.
